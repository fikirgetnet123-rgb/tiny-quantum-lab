Experiment 04 — Photoelectric effect

Why can bright red light still fail?

Shine light on a metal and watch electrons escape. Change the light's color and brightness to discover why the energy of each photon matters more than the total brightness.

Try this: turn red light all the way up. Can brightness alone release electrons?
0 photons sent · 0 electrons released

What did you notice?

Color sets the energy. Brightness sets how many photons arrive.

A blue photon has more energy than a red photon because blue light has a higher frequency. If each photon has less energy than the metal needs to release an electron, making the light brighter still does not make those photons energetic enough. This is one of the key pieces of evidence that light interacts with matter in discrete packets called photons.

what this animation is showing

Colored dots represent photons. An electron is released when an individual photon's energy is high enough to overcome the selected metal's work function. This is a simplified model of the photoelectric effect.

The idea, step by step

One photon must provide enough energy.

  1. 1

    Light comes in photons.

    Brightness controls how many photons arrive each second. It does not increase the energy of each photon.

  2. 2

    Color tells us the photon energy.

    Blue and violet light have higher frequencies than red light, so each blue or violet photon carries more energy.

  3. 3

    The electron needs enough energy to escape.

    A metal holds its electrons in place. The minimum energy needed to remove one is called the work function.

  4. 4

    Extra energy becomes kinetic energy.

    If the photon has more energy than the work function, the electron can escape. The remaining energy becomes the electron's kinetic energy.

The essential maths

E = hf

Photon energy E equals Planck's constant h times frequency f. Higher frequency means higher-energy photons.

K = hf − φ

φ is the metal's work function. If hf is smaller than φ, the photon cannot release an electron. If it is larger, the difference becomes the electron's kinetic energy K.

Make a change, see a reason

What do the controls actually do?

Shift toward blue

Each photon carries more energy. Once the photon energy reaches the metal's threshold, electrons can escape.

Increase brightness

More photons arrive each second. If their energy is above the threshold, more electrons can be released. If their energy is below the threshold, increasing brightness still does not release electrons.

Choose an easier metal

A lower work function means less energy is needed for an electron to escape.

Watch for the key surprise

In the photoelectric effect, many low-energy photons do not simply combine their energies to release one electron. Each photon must provide enough energy on its own.
