Experiment 03 — Wave packets

How can a quantum particle have a location and still behave like a wave?

A wave packet is a quantum state that is concentrated around a particular region of space. It can move, spread out, and reflect from a wall. Press play and watch the wavefunction and the probability of finding the particle change over time.

The wavefunction moves and spreads. The probability shows where the particle is likely to be found.

The idea, step by step

Why does a quantum wave packet spread?

A localized particle state cannot be made from just one wavelength. It needs many different wave components. These components have different momenta and evolve at different rates, so the packet gradually spreads out.

  1. 1

    One perfect wave is spread everywhere.

    A single infinite wave has one exact wavelength and therefore one exact momentum. But it extends across all of space, so it cannot describe a particle that is localized near one particular place.

  2. 2

    Many waves can combine into a packet.

    When waves with different wavelengths are added together, they can reinforce each other in one region and mostly cancel elsewhere. The result is a wave packet: a quantum state concentrated around a particular location.

  3. 3

    A narrower packet needs a wider range of momenta.

    To make the particle more localized, we need to combine more different wave components. That means a larger spread of possible momenta. These components evolve differently, so a narrow packet spreads faster.

  4. 4

    The packet can move while it spreads.

    The center of the probability distribution moves according to the particle's momentum. At the same time, the packet can become wider. If it reaches a wall, the quantum state reflects from it. A measurement still gives one definite position for the particle.

The essential maths

P(x) = |ψ(x)|2

This tells us the probability of detecting the particle at position x. The wavefunction is ψ, and its absolute value squared gives the probability density. The blue shape in the simulation represents this probability.

Δx · Δp ≥ ℏ / 2

This is the uncertainty principle. Δx describes the spread in position, while Δp describes the spread in momentum. It is not just a problem with our measuring tools. It is a basic property of quantum states. A particle that is very localized in position must have a larger spread of possible momenta.

Make a change, see a reason

What do the controls actually do?

Make the packet narrower

The particle starts more localized in space. But a narrow position spread requires a wider range of momenta, so the packet spreads more quickly.

Make the particle heavier

For the same kind of wave packet, a larger mass makes the packet spread more slowly. This is one reason quantum effects are much easier to notice for tiny particles than for everyday objects.

Add more momentum

The center of the packet moves faster in the direction of its momentum. Changing the momentum mainly changes how quickly the packet travels across the space.

Put a wall at the end

The packet reaches the wall and reflects. The quantum wavefunction changes as it reflects, and the probability distribution moves back into the available space.

Your mini investigation

Can you control how the packet spreads?

  1. Start with a wide packet in open space and watch it move for a while.
  2. Reset, make the packet narrow, and compare how quickly the blue probability curve spreads.
  3. Keep the packet narrow but make the particle heavier. What changes?
  4. Turn on the wall. Predict what will happen when the packet reaches it, then test your prediction.
