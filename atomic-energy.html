Experiment 05 — Atomic energy levels

Why do atoms glow in particular colors?

An electron in an atom can only occupy certain energy states. Give the atom energy and the electron can move to a higher state. When it returns to a lower state, the energy difference can be released as a photon with a specific color.

What did you notice?

An atom does not emit every color. Its allowed energies determine the colors.

Electrons in atoms can occupy specific energy levels rather than any energy they want. When an electron moves from a higher level to a lower one, the energy difference can be released as a photon. A larger energy difference means a higher-frequency, more energetic photon.

what this animation is showing

The rings represent allowed energy levels, not tiny planet-like orbits. The moving dot is a simple visual way to show the electron changing between energy states.

The idea, step by step

Atoms can produce a characteristic spectrum.

  1. 1

    Only certain energy levels are allowed.

    Quantum mechanics allows certain electron states around an atom and does not allow the states in between. Each allowed state has a specific energy.

  2. 2

    Energy can excite an electron.

    Energy from light, heat, collisions, or electricity can move an electron into a higher allowed state.

  3. 3

    The electron can return to a lower state.

    An excited electron can move back to a lower allowed state. When it does, the energy difference has to be accounted for.

  4. 4

    A photon can carry away the difference.

    When the energy difference is emitted as a photon, its frequency is set by that difference. Different transitions therefore produce different photon energies and colors.

The essential maths

ΔE = hf

The energy difference ΔE between two levels can be carried by a photon. Higher frequency f means a more energetic photon.

En ∝ −1/n2

For a simple hydrogen-like atom, higher level number n means a higher energy state and a less tightly bound electron. The important idea here is that the allowed energies are discrete, not continuous.

Make a change, see a reason

What do the controls actually do?

Choose a higher level

The electron starts from a higher energy state. Depending on the transition it makes, the energy difference can be larger, producing a more energetic photon.

Change atom type

Different atoms have different energy-level structures. Their allowed transitions can therefore produce different sets of photon energies and colors.

Change fall speed

This changes only how quickly the animation shows the transition. The photon's color is determined by the energy difference, not by the animation speed.

Look for the real-world link

The pattern of wavelengths in an atomic spectrum can identify an element. Astronomers use these spectral lines to learn what elements are present in stars and other distant objects.
